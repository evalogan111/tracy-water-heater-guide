Water Heater Service in Tracy, CA: ZIP Codes, Permits and Common Problems

Tracy grew fast, and a lot of its homes were built in the same few decades. That means many of their water heaters are aging on the same schedule, and owners are facing the same decisions.

Which Office Issues Your Permit

Homes Built in a Boom

Large parts of Tracy were built from the 1990s through the 2000s. A tank water heater installed when the house was new is likely past the 10-to-15-year life the Department of Energy gives for storage water heaters. If yours is original, it may already be on its second or third replacement cycle, or overdue for one.

Check the age on the rating label. If the manufacture date is not printed, it is coded in the serial number, and the manufacturer can decode it.

Common Problems in Tracy-Area Homes

Hot water runs out fast

Families grow in houses that were sized for fewer people. If your household has outgrown the heater, the fix is a unit with a higher first-hour rating, not a repair. Sediment and a failed lower element can cause the same symptom, so get a diagnosis first.

Garage heater on a platform

Many homes of this era have the gas heater in the garage, sometimes raised on a platform. When it is replaced, the new unit still needs proper strapping, venting and a relief valve line. Newer gas tank heaters are designed to resist igniting flammable vapors, which can change what the installation needs.

Switching to tankless

Some Tracy owners consider tankless when the old tank fails. It can work well, but the gas line serving a tank is often too small for a tankless unit, and the venting is different. Ask for those costs in the quote.

Water Quality

The City of Tracy publishes a yearly water quality report listing hardness, and Mountain House has its own water system and report. Harder water means more sediment and scale, so flush a tank about once a year and descale a tankless unit on schedule.

Utilities

PG&E supplies natural gas in the Tracy area. If you smell gas near the heater, leave the building on foot, then call 911 and PG&E from a safe distance. California requires carbon monoxide alarms in homes with fuel-burning appliances.

Before You Hire

  • Check the contractor's license with the Contractors State License Board.
  • Ask for the diagnosis and price in writing.
  • Confirm the permit, straps and relief valve line are included.
  • Ask about the warranty on parts and labor, in writing.
  • California generally limits the down payment on a home improvement contract to 10% of the price or $1,000, whichever is less.
